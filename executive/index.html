warehouse-systems · executive briefing · November 2026network-inventory-planning
Recommends stock transfers between warehouse sites: it builds a coherent planning snapshot from Kafka-fed facts (site capability, site-SKU demand, capacity plans), scores transfer proposals deterministically with reproducible inputs and reason codes, and — only after operator approval — runs a saga that asks inventory-storage to reserve origin stock, releases pick and dispatch work demand, and follows fulfillment and inventory facts to RECEIVED. It never becomes the inventory ledger and never moves physical stock itself; every recommendation stays advisory until an operator approves it and inventory-storage confirms a reservation.
what it does

Six capabilities take site facts all the way to a received transfer

Deterministic proposals

Stock targets, priority and lane costs give the same answer every time, in whole cases.

Fail-closed planning

Stale facts drop their site; closed lanes or negative scores stop planning.

Operator approval

Operators turn proposals into transfer plans, checked against current data.

Transfer saga

Approved plans reserve stock, release work, then follow facts to received.

Cancel with compensation

A transfer cancelled after allocation releases its reservation; later cancels are refused.

Rebalance policy and lanes

Versioned policy and lane catalogues drive observe-only runs and stuck-transfer checks.

what could go wrong · and what we do about it

Our biggest risk is quality gates and telemetry that trail the fleet

Read models are eventually consistent

Read models are eventually consistent; a stale fact excludes its site from the snapshot and approval is refused only when its origin…

→

Stale facts fail closed: approval is refused when its origin or destination site is excluded.

Every endpoint is unauthenticated, by decision

Every REST, MCP and A2A endpoint is unauthenticated BY DECISION (README 'No authentication', MCP tools.md…

→

Decision fleet-wide: the cluster-internal network boundary is the only access control.

Each Kafka consumer group is its own off switch

Each of the six Kafka consumer groups is its own off switch with no default: an unset group means that consumer silently does not run…

→

Group ids come from env with no default, so local runs never join the live cluster group.

Mutation gate is below the 99/99 fleet standard

Mutation gate 83/88 is below the 99/99 fleet standard; the blocking job covers two packages and the full-domain run is weekly/advisory…

→

The blocking mutation job covers two packages; the full-domain run is weekly and advisory.
